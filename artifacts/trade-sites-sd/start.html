Step two

Photos.

Your process photos are what prove you did the work. The final photo is what makes someone want to hire you. About 8 to 10 photos per job is plenty — three or four jobs documented well beats twenty documented badly.

The most important rule: same spot.

Before you start, stand in one place and take the BEFORE photo. Remember exactly where you stood. Every other wide photo of that space gets taken from that same spot — same position, same height, same angle.

When the photos line up, people can see the change instantly. When they do not line up, it just looks like three different rooms.

What to photograph, in order.

01

BEFORE — before you touch anything

  • Stand back and get the whole space, not a close-up
  • Show the damage, the bare walls, the old surface — whatever you were hired to fix
  • Take 2 shots so you have a backup
02

PROCESS — one photo per service, as you go

  • Once it is finished and painted, nobody can tell how good the work underneath was
  • Shoot at the point where your work is clearly visible but not yet covered up
  • Three services on one job means three process photos
03

FINAL — when the job is done and cleaned up

  • Same spot as the BEFORE
  • Everything picked up: no tape, no plastic, no drop cloths, no tools, no ladder
  • Lights on, curtains open. One good final photo covers every service you did.
04

OUTSIDE — the front of the house or building

  • One photo from the street or driveway
  • Do not include house numbers, license plates, or mailboxes with names on them

Every single time.

  • Clean up first. Remove tape, plastic, drop cloths, tools, buckets, fans, ladders. A messy photo makes good work look sloppy.
  • Phone sideways (horizontal), not upright.
  • Turn on the lights and open the curtains. No flash — it flattens the wall and hides your work.
  • Tap the screen on the wall so it focuses before you shoot.
  • Hold it level. Walls should look straight up and down, not tilted.
  • No people's faces, no personal belongings, no mail, no family photos on the wall.
  • Send by text message, not WhatsApp. WhatsApp shrinks photos and they come out blurry.

Optional, and also useful.

The fully furnished photo

Powerful when the room gets furniture back and looks great. Only take it if you are the one moving the furniture back, or the homeowner says yes. Never photograph inside someone's furnished home without asking — if unsure, skip it.

Close-ups of detail work

Two or three per job — a clean corner, a straight edge, a smooth seam.

One photo of you working

With your tools. This goes in the about section of your site, and it is the photo people connect with most.

Step three

Reviews from past customers.

We need 3 to 5 short reviews from people you have worked for. They go next to your photos, and they matter more than anything you say about yourself. Text or call people you did good work for — you probably already know who they are. Most say yes; it takes them a minute.

Send them this message

Hey [name], hope you're doing well. I'm putting together a website to show my work, and I'd like to include a few words from people I've worked for. Could you send me 2 or 3 sentences about the job I did and how it went? No rush, and thank you either way.

When they reply, send us:

  • What they wrote
  • Their first name and last initial
  • Their city or neighborhood
  • What the job was

Privacy: we never publish full names, addresses, or phone numbers. If someone already left you a review on Google or Facebook, just send us the link — we can use that too.
